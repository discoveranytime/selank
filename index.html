Selank
the calm peptide

A 7-amino-acid peptide built from tuftsin — a natural immune-system messenger — with the same stabilizer tail as Semax. Developed in Russia as an anti-anxiety medicine without sedation, registered there for clinical use, and unknown to Western regulators. Here is what it actually is, separated from the hype.

Quick read

Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro) is a synthetic analog of tuftsin, extended with Pro-Gly-Pro for stability. Russian research describes anxiety relief without drowsiness, steadier stress hormones, and even immune and memory effects. It has been used clinically in Russia for years — but human data is small, Russian-language, and never replicated in the West, and gray-market vials are unregulated. Scroll for the full picture.

01The molecule

What Selank actually is

Selank starts from an unlikely place: your immune system. Its parent molecule, tuftsin, is a tiny messenger that rallies immune cells. Russian scientists took that four-block fragment, stabilized it the same way they stabilized Semax, and got a calming peptide.

Each bead is one amino acid. The front half is borrowed from immunity; the tail is engineering.

Built on tuftsin

Tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) is a natural immune peptide your body already makes. Selank is tuftsin plus the Pro-Gly-Pro tail — the same stabilization trick as Semax.

Calm, not sedation

The headline claim: anxiety relief without drowsiness or addiction risk — the feature that separates it from benzodiazepines in Russian descriptions.

Medicine in Russia, chemical elsewhere

A registered anxiolytic in Russia (nasal spray). Everywhere else: unregulated research chemical — same name, no oversight.

Plain English

If Semax is a photocopied stress memo, Selank is a borrowed immune-system text message, rewritten to calm the brain. Same laminating trick (the PGP tail), completely different original message — which is why the two are constantly discussed as a pair.

02Immune roots

The tuftsin story starts with white blood cells, not neurons. How an immune fragment became Russia's answer to anxiety is one of the stranger paths in pharmacology.

From blood protein to brain drug: the strangest career change in pharmacology.

Tuftsin's day job

Tuftsin rallies immune cells — it's part of how your body coordinates its defenses. Nobody set out to make an anti-anxiety drug from it; the calming effects showed up in behavioral testing.

The TP-7 detour

In the scientific literature Selank appears as TP-7. It was explicitly designed as a stable, longer-acting tuftsin analog — the anxiety angle was the discovery, not the starting goal.

The benzo comparison

Russian trials positioned Selank against benzodiazepines for anxiety — claiming similar calm with no sedation, no withdrawal, no addiction. Big claims from small trials.

Plain English

Selank is a career-change molecule: hired by the immune system, poached by brain researchers, now working the anxiety beat in Russian pharmacies. Whether it does the job well is exactly what the evidence chapter is for.

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

Like Semax, Selank's human file is real, Russian, and hard to audit — with the added twist that anxiety is notoriously placebo-responsive.

Russian clinical use

Registered anxiolytic in Russia, studied for generalized anxiety and stress disorders. Real clinical experience — inside one country's system.

Small trials, soft endpoints

Human studies are small and mostly Russian-language. Anxiety is measured by questionnaires — the endpoint most vulnerable to placebo and expectation.

No Western replication

No Western randomized trials exist. The entire human case for Selank rests on studies outsiders can't easily verify.

Proposed vs demonstrated

ProposedThe theory

  • Calm without sedation — anxiety relief that leaves you functional.
  • Serotonin steadiness — normalizes serotonin metabolism rather than flooding it.
  • GABA support — strengthens the brain's inhibitory, calming signaling.
  • BDNF & memory protection — supports neuron health and learning.
  • Immune modulation — shifts cytokine signaling via its tuftsin heritage.
  • Clean focus — clearer thinking as a side effect of less anxiety.

DemonstratedWhat's actually shown

  • Anxiety-like behavior drops in rodents — consistent across animal models, without sedation.
  • Small Russian trials report anxiolytic effects — real, but questionnaire-based and hard to audit.
  • Registered medicine in Russia — genuine clinical use inside one country's system.
  • Serotonin & BDNF shifts seen in animals — mechanism hints, not human proof.
  • Human GABA mechanism: unproven. The headline mechanism has no direct human demonstration.
  • Cognitive enhancement in humans: unproven. "Focus" may just be anxiety relief.

Anxiety relief is the easiest thing in medicine to feel and the hardest to prove — placebo melts anxiety too. Selank's file is a stack of Russian questionnaires saying it works, with no independent referee. Believable? Maybe. Proven? Not to Western standards.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

Marketed as the gentle one — but "gentle" is a claim, not a safety file. What's known, what's theoretical, and what's blank.

Unregulated vials

Observed fact: Outside Russia, what's sold as Selank has no quality oversight. Independent testing of gray-market peptides has repeatedly found wrong doses, impurities, and mislabeled vials.

Mild but real side effects

Observed fact: Russian labeling and reports mention nasal irritation, headache, fatigue, occasional irritability — mild, but "no side effects" is marketing, not data.

Immune-system meddling

Theoretical: Selank shifts cytokine and chemokine gene expression (its tuftsin heritage). Deliberately nudging immunity long-term is poorly understood territory.

The enkephalinase question

Theoretical: Like Semax, Selank is proposed to slow breakdown of the brain's own opioid peptides — a second pharmacology that gets little attention in marketing.

Dependence & withdrawal

Unknown: "Non-addictive" is claimed from short Russian courses. No long-term dependence studies exist in Western literature.

Pregnancy, interactions

Unknown: No data in pregnancy, no formal drug-interaction studies. The label for these questions is blank.

Plain English

Known: mild side effects, unregulated vials. Theoretical: immune and opioid-system side quests nobody fully mapped. Unknown: long-term use. "No sedation" is a real advantage over benzos — if the underlying data holds up.

10Glossary

Understanding Selank Terminology

Twelve terms, each with the science and a plain-English translation. Tap any term to expand it.

The scienceTuftsin is a naturally occurring tetrapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg) produced in the body during the breakdown of antibodies, where it stimulates immune cells such as macrophages and neutrophils. It functions as a small immune-signaling fragment, rallying phagocytic cells to engulf pathogens and debris. Selank is a synthetic, stabilized analogue of tuftsin, extended with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail — the same engineering used in Semax. Its development effectively repurposed an immune peptide for research into anxiety and cognition.

Plain EnglishTuftsin is a tiny immune peptide — four building blocks — that your body makes when it breaks down antibodies, and it rallies immune cells like macrophages to attack invaders. Selank is a lab-made, toughened-up version of tuftsin with a protective tail added. So Selank started life as an immune-system messenger and got repurposed for anxiety research. Same family as Semax, different job application.

The scienceTP-7 is the laboratory code name for Selank used in the scientific literature — the designation for the tuftsin analogue extended with Pro-Gly-Pro, developed at Moscow’s Zakusov Institute of Pharmacology. Like most research-stage compounds, it circulated under an alphanumeric code before acquiring its common name. Papers indexed under TP-7 describe the same molecule sold and discussed as Selank. The dual naming is worth knowing when searching the evidence base.

Plain EnglishTP-7 is Selank’s formal lab name — the code researchers used in scientific papers for the tuftsin-plus-tail molecule from Moscow’s Zakusov Institute. Before compounds get common names, they go by codes like this. If you’re digging through research, papers filed under TP-7 are talking about Selank. Same molecule, more formal paperwork.

The scienceA heptapeptide is a peptide chain composed of exactly seven amino acids linked in sequence. Seven residues is a practical middle ground in peptide chemistry: long enough to fold into a shape a receptor or enzyme can recognize specifically, yet short enough to synthesize cheaply and consistently. Selank’s seven residues are the four of tuftsin plus the three of the Pro-Gly-Pro tail. Most peptide drugs fall in the 5–40 amino acid range, so heptapeptides sit at the compact end.

Plain EnglishA heptapeptide is a protein chain exactly seven building blocks long. Seven is a sweet spot: big enough to carry a specific message, small enough to manufacture cheaply and reliably. Selank’s seven are tuftsin’s four plus the three-block protective tail. Most peptide drugs run 5 to 40 blocks, so seven is compact.

The sciencePro-Gly-Pro is a three-amino-acid sequence (proline–glycine–proline) grafted onto the end of both Selank and Semax. Peptides are normally destroyed within minutes by peptidases, the enzymes that cut protein chains — but proline’s rigid ring structure fits poorly into those enzymes’ cutting sites, making this tail unusually resistant to breakdown. Researchers added it to extend Selank’s working life from minutes toward hours. The shared tail is why Selank and Semax are often called sister molecules.

Plain EnglishPro-Gly-Pro is a three-block tail (proline–glycine–proline) bolted onto the end of both Selank and Semax. Natural peptides get shredded by enzymes within minutes, but proline’s stiff ring shape resists those cutting tools — so the tail acts like armor. It was added to stretch the molecule’s working life from minutes toward hours. This shared tail is why Selank and Semax are called sister molecules.

The scienceAn anxiolytic is a substance that reduces anxiety. The classic anxiolytics — benzodiazepines — are effective but carry sedation, dependence, and withdrawal, because they broadly amplify GABA signaling. Selank is reported in Russian clinical studies to reduce anxiety without the sedation and dependence associated with benzodiazepines, which is the central claim behind its development. That claim rests on small trials and has not been validated by large, modern, internationally regulated studies.

Plain EnglishAn anxiolytic is anything that reduces anxiety. The classic ones — benzodiazepines like Valium — work well but sedate you and can hook you, because they crank up the brain’s brake pedal. Selank’s big selling point is calming anxiety without the fog or the addiction risk, based on small Russian trials. That “calm without the cost” claim is the whole pitch — and it hasn’t been proven in large modern trials.

The scienceSerotonin is a neurotransmitter that regulates mood, anxiety, sleep, and appetite, and it is the target of many anti-anxiety and antidepressant drugs. The serotonin system is deeply involved in the brain’s threat-and-calm circuitry. Selank is reported to normalize aspects of serotonin metabolism in animal studies — for example, countering stress-induced drops in brain serotonin. These findings are preclinical and mechanistic; they do not establish that Selank treats anxiety in humans through this route.

Plain EnglishSerotonin is a brain chemical that helps regulate mood, anxiety, sleep, and appetite — it’s what most anti-anxiety and antidepressant drugs target. Selank is reported to steady serotonin levels in animal studies, for instance reversing stress-driven drops in the brain. That’s a plausible piece of the calmness story. But it’s animal data — it doesn’t prove how, or whether, Selank works in people.

The scienceGABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the brain’s primary inhibitory neurotransmitter — its main “brake pedal,” quieting neural activity throughout the central nervous system. Boosting GABA signaling is how benzodiazepines and alcohol produce their calming (and sedating) effects. Selank is reported to modulate GABAergic signaling more gently, which is proposed as part of its anxiolytic action without heavy sedation. As with its other mechanisms, the evidence comes from animal and cell studies.

Plain EnglishGABA is the brain’s brake pedal — the main chemical that quiets neural activity and keeps the brain from over-firing. Benzos and alcohol calm you by slamming this brake, which is also why they sedate you. Selank is said to nudge the same brake more gently — calming without knocking you out. That’s the theory, built on animal and cell studies.

The scienceBDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) is a growth protein that supports neuron survival, encourages new neural connections, and strengthens the synapses involved in learning and memory. It is one of the most studied molecules in neuroscience; low BDNF is associated with depression, chronic stress, and cognitive decline. Selank is reported to regulate BDNF expression in memory-related brain regions in animal studies. Like the rest of Selank’s mechanism story, this finding is preclinical and its relevance to human cognition is unproven.

Plain EnglishBDNF is a growth protein that helps brain cells survive, form new connections, and strengthen memory circuits — one of the most studied brain molecules there is. The scienceCytokines are small signaling proteins the immune system uses to coordinate inflammation and defense — the text messages immune cells send each other. They include interferons, interleukins, and tumor necrosis factor, and their balance determines whether inflammation resolves or runs out of control. Selank is reported to alter the expression of genes for several cytokines, a legacy of its tuftsin immune-peptide origins. This immunomodulatory angle is studied in cell and animal models.

The scienceIntranasal delivery is the absorption of a drug through the nasal lining, a thin, blood-rich membrane that sits close to the brain. Russian Selank was formulated as a nasal spray for this reason. The route’s drawbacks are practical: congestion, technique, and mucus all change the delivered dose, and gray-market products add unknown concentration and sterility.

The scienceA placebo effect is improvement caused by expectation rather than by the treatment itself, and it is strongest for subjective outcomes — anxiety, mood, perceived calm — exactly the territory of Selank anecdotes. Expectation measurably changes brain chemistry: dopamine and the body’s own opioids rise when a person believes they received something active. Online communities amplify the effect through enthusiastic reports and the ritual of dosing. This does not mean every Selank story is fabricated; it means felt calm cannot distinguish drug from belief without blinding.

Plain EnglishA placebo effect is feeling calmer because you expected to, not because the substance did anything. It’s strongest exactly where Selank stories live: anxiety, mood, the feeling of calm. Expectation physically changes brain chemistry — belief alone raises dopamine and natural opioids. Forums amplify it with glowing reports and daily dosing rituals. The sciencePreclinical research is the stage of drug investigation conducted in cell cultures and animal models before any human is exposed. It is the necessary first stretch of drug development, testing toxicity and mechanism — and it is also where most drug candidates die, with only a small fraction of animal successes ever becoming approved human medicines.
